### Notes

#### L1 — Lookback Lens licensing
The [Lookback Lens](https://github.com/voidism/Lookback-Lens) repository has **no `LICENSE` file at its root**; its README displays an MIT badge. The stated intent therefore appears to be MIT; in any case, here we link to the Lookback Lens files rather than redistributing them. The annotation and lookback-ratio files are additionally derivatives of the underlying NQ and CNN/DailyMail content (see below), whose own terms also apply.

#### D1 — Movies (via LLMsKnow)
The `movie_qa_*` CSVs are constructed by [LLMsKnow](https://github.com/technion-cs-nlp/LLMsKnow), which is the immediate source we obtain them from. Per the LLMsKnow paper, the prompts ("Who acted as [figure] in the movie [movie]?") and their answers were sourced from the Kaggle ["The Movies Dataset"](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rounakbanik/the-movies-dataset) by `rounakbanik`, which is listed as public-domain/CC0 on Kaggle. That Kaggle dataset is itself an ensemble: movie details, credits, and keywords come from the **TMDB Open API**, and links/ratings from **GroupLens/MovieLens**. The actor/movie content used derives from the TMDB portion. Note that TMDB and GroupLens/MovieLens data carries its own terms of use; both apply to the underlying content independently of LLMsKnow's MIT license.

#### D2 — AnswerableMath (via LLMsKnow)
The `AnswerableMath*` CSVs are provided within [LLMsKnow](https://github.com/technion-cs-nlp/LLMsKnow), which is the immediate source we obtain them from. Their origin is the **UMWP (Unanswerable Math Word Problem)** benchmark (["Benchmarking Hallucination in Large Language Models based on Unanswerable Math Word Problem", Sun et al., LREC-COLING 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.196/); [arXiv:2403.03558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03558)), with code and data at [github.com/Yuki-Asuuna/UMWP](https://github.com/Yuki-Asuuna/UMWP). That repository licenses its **code under Apache-2.0** and its **data under CC BY-SA 4.0** (stated explicitly in its README). UMWP was in turn constructed by human annotators from pre-existing math-word-problem datasets (e.g. SVAMP, MultiArith), which carry their own licenses.
